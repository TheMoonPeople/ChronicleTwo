## What the unused v44 is

A fresh capture of the current draft (`.private/nmmisc/mv/v/cur.*`) matches the
earlier numbering: wrap-branch load CSE temporaries `n` v43, `d4madr` v45,
`size` v46, `data` v47, and the address mask v36 after every CSE temporary.
Numbers v44 (wrap) and v39 (else) are allocated but have no definitions or
uses.

Removing the wrap branch's CHCR mask (diagnostic only) closes the gap: the
CSE temporaries become `n` 42, `d4madr` 43, `size` 44, `data` 45, and the tag
mask becomes the immediate `dsll32`/`dsrl32` pair. v44 is therefore the CSE
temporary of the `0x0FFFFFFF` constant shared by `DmaAddr(buf->tag)` and the
CHCR mask. MWCC later deletes that temporary and creates v36 for the
register use. That is the one slot retail's ordering needs (between v43 and
v45). Re-simulating the captured graph with the mask at v44 reproduces
retail once `n` loses any one of its ordinary interferences (every neighbour
except the mask and `d4madr` works); adding a one-degree neighbour does not.
Retail thus implies a surviving constant CSE temporary plus one shorter
overlap with the ring count.

Small compiles of two-use masks (`.private/nmmisc/mv/mini/m1`, `m3`, `m5`)
show the same rule seen here: a lone `x & 0x0FFFFFFF` becomes `dsll32`/`dsrl32`,
and with two uses at most one gets a `lui`/`ori` register while the others stay
immediate. Which use gets the register depends on context and no rule was
isolated: the first use in some store pairs (`f4`, `f5`, `a3`), neither use in
other store pairs (`a1`, `f8`) or when the results feed a call (`m5`), and the
middle use of three (`a5`).

## Level 4 runs the optimizer twice

`mwccps2.exe` derives one flag from `optimization_level 4` (byte `0x57DBF4`,
set at `0x43B008`; the level itself is `0x57DBE5`). Its only reader in the
optimizer driver is `0x43AB56`: the IRO pass sequence runs twice at level 4,
once otherwise. Each round begins with copy and constant propagation
(`0x4CAB60`), followed later by `IRO_CommonSubs` (`0x4C48A0`/`0x4C4FC0`/`0x4C5520`).
No individual `opt_*` pragma reproduces or suppresses the second round
(sweeps at level 3 with each turned on, and at level 4 with the rest turned off,
leave 49 and 9 words).

This explains the draft's numbering. Round-1 CSE creates the mask temporary
between `n` and `d4madr` (v44). Round-2 propagation folds that constant
assignment away, and round-2 CSE creates v36, numbered after every surviving
round-1 temporary. A level-3 capture (`.private/nmmisc/mv/v/l3.*`) keeps the
round-1 temporary: wrap `n` v40, mask v41, `d4madr` v42. The mask has only the
tag use and the CHCR mask stays an immediate pair, as in retail. Level 3 also
loses retail's level-4 code elsewhere (the hoisted `ipu_ctrl` address in the
first busy-wait and the delay-slot `n - 1`), 49 words in all.

Retail is therefore level-4 code whose round-1 mask temporary survives the
second propagation, plus one fewer interference for `n`. No source form tried
keeps a constant CSE temporary through round 2. The propagation candidate
checks begin at `0x4CA990` and were not decoded further.

Captures of the level-4 pragma variants locate the deletion. With
`opt_propagation off` the round-1 temporary survives in retail's slot (wrap
`n` v42, mask v43, `d4madr` v44), but it keeps both uses, so the CHCR mask
becomes a register `and` (163 words). With `opt_loop_invariants off` or
`opt_dead_assignments off` the mask is still recreated after every CSE
temporary (v36 or v37). Copy and constant propagation is therefore what
removes the round-1 temporary. At level 3, the propagation that follows
round-1 CSE replaces only the CHCR use with the immediate and leaves the tag
use on the temporary; at level 4, the propagation that opens round 2 replaces
the tag use as well.
